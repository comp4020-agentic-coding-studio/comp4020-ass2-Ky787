## The brief

> Here is a function with opaque control flow, a recovered control-flow graph,
> a set of frozen solver results, and a one-paragraph claim that somebody has
> made about it. Decide whether the claim is supported. Show your working.

A1 asked what happened to a program. A2 asks a harder question: what does this
pile of evidence actually entitle anyone to say?

## What you are given

A bundle containing, for one target function:

- the exact disassembly of the target, and the clean comparator's;
- a recovered control-flow graph, with its counting convention stated;
- frozen solver artefacts for several branches — the path prefix, the branch
  condition, both queries as `.smt2` files, and the recorded status of each;
- the retained runtime output of the binary;
- **a claim**, written as an analyst's summary paragraph, which may be
  supported, partly supported, or wrong.

One of the branches in your bundle is a genuine source condition. Finding it is
part of the work; a submission that treats every branch as injected has missed
the control that makes the rest meaningful.

## What you submit

One PDF, six to eight pages:

1. **The function.** What it appears to compute, and the evidence for that.
2. **Branch by branch.** For each branch you discuss: the path prefix `P`, the
   condition `C`, the two obligations `P ∧ C` and `P ∧ ¬C`, the recorded status
   of each, and your classification — one-sided under `P`, two-sided, or
   undetermined. Quote the file.
3. **The assumptions.** What the analysis assumed about memory, about globals,
   and about the initial state. For each assumption, one sentence on what
   changes if it does not hold.
4. **The graph.** What the recovered graph tells you and what its convention
   excludes. If you quote block counts, they must be within one convention.
5. **The verdict.** Is the supplied claim supported? Answer plainly — yes,
   partly, or no — then say which specific words in it go beyond the evidence,
   and what evidence would settle it.

## How it is marked

Two failure modes are worth naming in advance, because they are the ones that
cost the most marks.

The first is **collapsing a path-local result into a global one**. `UNSAT` for
`P ∧ ¬C` says that, for states covered by `P` and under the analysis's model,
that edge is not taken. It does not say the branch is universally opaque. The
course has a specimen where a workflow yields zero proofs from arbitrary entry
state and three from three once a captured initialised state is imported —
same solver, same branches, different assumption. If your submission cannot
express that distinction, it cannot adjudicate the claim.

The second is **reading the graph as the answer**. A recovered graph is a
recovery; it is incomplete at indirect jumps by construction, and its block
count belongs to one convention. Two of this course's specimens exist
specifically because a smaller graph was wrong.

Marks are for the reasoning, not for agreeing with the marker. A well-argued
"partly supported, and here is the sentence that is doing the overreaching"
scores above a correct verdict with thin working.

## The brief

> You are given a clean binary and an obfuscated binary built from the same
> frozen source, plus the compiler IR for both. Pick one arithmetic operation.
> Say what happened to it, in three representations, and say what you have not
> shown.

Everything you need was demonstrated in weeks 1 to 4 on specimens you have
already read. What makes this an assessment rather than an exercise is that
your pair is one you have not seen, and that half the marks are for the part
most people skip.

## What you are given

A private bundle in the course lab, containing:

- the frozen `showcase.c` you already have;
- one clean build and one obfuscated build of it, as post-pass LLVM IR and as
  exact disassembly of the named target function;
- the build command for each, including the optimisation level, the pass
  selection and the seed;
- the retained runtime output of both binaries.

Your pair is drawn from the same corpus this course teaches from, but it is not
one of the specimens on this site. Which tool produced it is stated in the
bundle — this is not a guessing game, and identifying the vendor earns nothing.

## What you submit

One PDF, four to six pages, in this order:

1. **The operation.** Which source operation you chose, and where it is: file
   and line in the C, value name in the IR, address in the disassembly.
2. **The transformation.** What the pass did, as algebra. If it is an identity,
   state it and verify it modulo 2³². If some of the emitted computation is
   dead, say which and how you know.
3. **The backend.** What changed between the post-pass IR and the machine code.
   This section is where most of the interesting findings are.
4. **The measurement.** Any counts you quote, each with its representation, its
   convention and the comparator you took it against. A count without all three
   is worth nothing here.
5. **What this does not establish.** At least four statements, each one a
   plausible conclusion that your evidence does *not* support, with one
   sentence on why not. Generic disclaimers do not count; these must be about
   your specimen.

No tool is required beyond a text editor. If you use a disassembler, a
decompiler or a simplifier, say which and at what version, and treat its output
as a claim you now have to support rather than as a result.

## How it is marked

The four criteria below are applied to the submission as a whole. The one worth
dwelling on is the third, because it is unusual and it is deliberate.

Twenty-five per cent of this assignment is for correctly refusing to conclude
things. A submission that traces the operation flawlessly and then asserts that
the obfuscation "makes the binary hard to analyse" has lost those marks: it has
not measured difficulty, it has measured length. A submission that traces the
operation adequately and then explains precisely why its own instruction count
is not a difficulty measure, why its `-O0` finding says nothing about `-O2`,
and which of its numbers are not comparable to each other, will do well.

For this assignment you are working entirely in the first two columns. Nothing

## Declaring your working

Collaboration on *reading* the specimens is encouraged and normal; the written
analysis is individual. If you discussed a finding with someone, name them.

If you used a disassembler, a decompiler or a simplifier, name it and its
version, and treat what it told you as a claim you now have to support. That is
the same standard this assignment marks you against, applied to your own
evidence.

Runs the binary-analysis group that produced this course's corpus: the frozen
`showcase.c`, the sixty-eight build conditions of the cross-obfuscator matrix,
and the proof-and-rewrite pipeline that turned twenty-two solver results into
six changed bytes.

Also responsible for the two results the course is most proud of, both of them
failures. The Polaris control-flow-flattening rewrite that returns the wrong
answer from a smaller graph, and the whole-function lift that produces
verifier-valid LLVM IR and miscomputes 3,917 of 4,118 inputs, are on this site
because they were kept rather than quietly replaced with specimens that
behaved. A course that only shows you what worked teaches you to expect it.

Interested in supervising work on indirect-dispatch recovery, on preserving
legitimate switch semantics through an unflattener, and on rewrite metadata
that a debugger can still be trusted with.

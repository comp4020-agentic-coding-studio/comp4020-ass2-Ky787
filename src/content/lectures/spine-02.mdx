## The argument

Obfuscated arithmetic gets simpler on its way to you, and usually not because
of anything you did.

Three separate mechanisms are at work, and the whole block is about telling
them apart. The **algebra** can be reduced: a mixed Boolean-arithmetic
expression has a simpler equivalent, and a synthesiser plus a solver can
sometimes find it and prove it. The **compiler backend** reduces it: an
obfuscator that expanded an addition into four IR operations still has
instruction selection to get past, and instruction selection is very good at
recognising an addition. And **you** reduce it, by reading the listing, which
feels like analysis and produces no artefact anyone can check.

The reason this matters is a specific, embarrassing failure mode. If a native
expression was *already* canonical before your simplifier ran, and your
simplifier reports it as simple, you have learned nothing about your
simplifier. The retained arithmetic study is careful about this and says so:
some expressions were recognisable before the tool ran, and those must not be
credited as recovered semantics.

## The numbers that frame the block

On the exact same target function, `demo_substitution`, at `-O0`:

- classic substitution grows the native body from 115 to 311 instructions, and
  by `-O1` all twenty-four of the intended operations are back as plain machine
  instructions;
- one implementation's substitution disappears entirely by `-O1` — the measured
  target matches the clean target;
- linear MBA grows the same body to 678 instructions with a distinct operator
  tree at every site;
- and a source-level arithmetic encoding still sits at 226 instructions against
  a matched clean 106 after the most aggressive optimisation the study ran.

Same function. Same source. Four completely different answers to "did this
survive?", and the deciding variable is mostly *where the transformation was
applied*, not how clever it was.

## Where this block ends

Week 4 is expression recovery and its honest scoreboard: ten of twelve isolated
sites recovered with proofs, two returned UNKNOWN, and not one of twenty
composed whole-function expressions completely recovered. Week 5 makes
optimisation level a variable rather than a nuisance. Week 6 moves from
arithmetic to control flow and introduces the distinction the whole second half
of the course runs on — a branch the programmer wrote, versus a branch an

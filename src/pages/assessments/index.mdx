There is no exam. An exam would test whether you can recognise a technique,
and recognising techniques is the part of this subject that generalises worst.

What the three assessments test instead is the reasoning: given evidence, what
follows, what does not, and what would you have to check. The weights climb
because the judgement required climbs — A1 gives you a pair and asks what
happened, A2 gives you a pile of results and a claim and asks whether the claim
holds, and the capstone gives you a sample and no help at all.

## Weekly work, which is not marked

Every week has a studio exercise on its page. None of them is graded and none
of them is collected.

That is a deliberate choice rather than a kindness. The studios are where you
get something wrong in front of somebody who can tell you, and a graded studio
turns that into a performance. The exercises feed the assessments directly —
A1 is week 3's exercise on a pair you have not seen, A2 is week 7's, and the
capstone is week 12's — so the practice is not optional in any sense that
matters. It just is not worth marks.

## Late work, extensions and integrity

Extensions are arranged with the convenor **before** the deadline and are
routine when asked for in time. Work submitted late without one is not
accepted.

Reading the evidence together is encouraged. Writing the analysis is
individual. Declare any collaboration and name the people involved — nobody
has ever lost a mark for declaring it. Tool output is not yours and is not
evidence on its own: if a simplifier, decompiler or lifter produced something
you rely on, name the tool and its version and treat its output as a claim you
now have to support. That rule is not an integrity technicality here, it is the
subject of the course.

Full rules, including the marking scale and the appeals route, are on the
[course information](/policies/) page.
